One program

From pattern to sewing line

One record follows the style through the cutting room. Each step uses the result of the step before, so numbers are not typed twice.

  1. Step 1

    Pattern

    Draft, grade and digitize patterns. Import DXF-AAMA or a supported Seamly2D file.

    BETA Pattern
  2. Step 2

    Marker

    Nest all pieces on the fabric width with grain, nap, pairs and size ratio. Edit by hand.

    BETA Marker
  3. Step 3

    Cut plan

    Turn an order into size ratios, ply heights and lay lengths.

    BETA Marker
  4. Step 4

    Rolls

    Inspect rolls (4-point), group shade and shrinkage, allocate rolls to lays.

    BETA Marker
  5. Step 5

    Spread

    Print spreading sheets with roll order, plies, splice zones and end allowances.

    Floor
  6. Step 6

    Cut

    Plot the marker 1:1 (PLT/HPGL or PDF) or send a cutter file. Track each lay.

    Floor
  7. Step 7

    Bundles

    Number plies, make bundles and print QR tickets and stickers. Plan re-cuts.

    Floor
  8. Step 8

    Sewing

    Scan bundles out to sewing lines. Export work-in-progress and fabric use.

    Floor

Features

What it does today

Each of these runs on your own computer. The heavy work (nesting, checks, file output) is written in Rust for speed.

Pattern making

  • Drafting with measurements and formulas
  • Grading and size checks
  • Digitize a paper pattern from a photo
  • DXF-AAMA export; Seamly2D file subset import

Marker making

  • Automatic nesting with garment rules: grain, one-way nap, left/right pairs, size ratio, gaps
  • Folded and tubular fabric, shade bands, fabric defects
  • Manual editing: bump, compact, swap, sections, marker library
  • Every marker is checked by an independent validator before export

Cut planning and costing

  • Size ratios, ply heights and lay lengths from an order
  • Fabric consumption and cost estimates
  • Compare fabric widths (command line)

Rolls and spreading

  • 4-point roll inspection and defect maps
  • Shade and shrinkage groups
  • Roll allocation, splice zones and end bits

Bundles and the floor

  • Ply numbering, bundles, QR tickets and stickers
  • Re-cut planning for damaged parts
  • Bundle scan-out to sewing lines with a USB scanner

Files and data

  • Plot: PLT/HPGL and 1:1 PDF; SVG, DXF, generic ISO cutter file
  • Import orders and rolls from CSV or Excel
  • Export consumption and work-in-progress as CSV

Not yet

We prefer to tell you before you find out.

  • Plotter and cutter profiles are generic. Test them on your machines before production.
  • Folded and tubular fabric work. Folded results improved a lot in 0.5 (T-shirt test 43% to 63%) but are still below open fabric.
  • No direct Tally or SAP connector yet. Exchange data with CSV or Excel files.
  • The server mode serves one computer in version 0.5. Shared use over the factory network is planned.
  • The Windows installer is not code-signed yet. Windows may show a warning.
  • Hindi and Tamil are drafts and need native review.

Privacy

Your data stays in your factory

BETA runs on computers you control. There is no BETA cloud to send your patterns to.

  • Patterns, markers, orders and roll data stay on your PCs and your server.
  • No account, no tracking, no usage data and no crash reports sent anywhere.
  • Works with no internet. Install and update from a USB drive.
  • The server listens only on its own computer unless you turn on factory network access with a token.

Who it is for

Garment factories

Cutting rooms that want control of their own data and no per-seat licence fees. First users are in India.

CAD and marker operators

People who make markers every day and need grain, nap, pairs, ratios, folds and shade rules to be right.

Merchandisers and planners

Fabric consumption, width comparison and cut plans from the same marker the cutting room uses.

Factory IT

A Windows installer, a command-line server and plain files. No cloud service to approve.

Researchers and developers

Open code, open benchmark and open data formats. Improve the nesting engine and prove it.

Send us your markers

We want to compare BETA with your current CAD on your own styles: same pieces, same fabric width, same rules. You get the results, and they stay private.

What to send

  1. 3 to 5 real styles exported as DXF-AAMA or ASTM from your CAD (for example a shirt, trouser, T-shirt or jeans).
  2. For each style, the marker your CAD made: fabric width, size ratio, rules (nap, 0/180, gaps), marker length and efficiency, and how long the auto-nest ran.
  3. If you can, one plotter file (PLT) from your cutting room, so we can check the plot scale on your plotter.

Your files stay private

We never commit, publish or share your patterns or markers without your written permission. Published results name no style, buyer or factory unless you agree.
