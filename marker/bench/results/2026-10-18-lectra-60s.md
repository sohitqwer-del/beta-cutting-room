# Lectra 60-second comparison — 18 October 2026

**Assumption:** `sheet_type=0` means an ordinary rectangular strip of `sheet_width`, with open single-layer fabric. No public codebook verifies this. `flip_x=1` is interpreted as x → −x before rotation. Tier A is exact conditional on these assumptions. Tier B includes Tier A and restricts each part to a subset of Lectra’s rotations and mirror states.

Lectra’s recorded efficiencies came from its own nesting engine on real client tasks. The paper gives a nominal five-minute budget. Hardware, engine version and worker count are unknown. These runs compare quality at selected search budgets; they do not establish hardware-equivalent speed or full garment-rule coverage.

The local machine was shared. Workspace verification overlapped early 60-second trials, and background CPU load was not controlled. Paired time differences also include stochastic scheduling and load effects.

Tier B fixes one handedness per physical part. It keeps the largest regular 1/2/4-angle subset or continuous rotation expressible with one shared fabric tilt. The converter maximizes summed allowed angular measure, then base-angle count. It records original and retained sets for every sampled part. Other constraint classes, extra parameters and unverified multi-ring shapes remain excluded. A positive sheet length is enforced as max_length.

Differences are BETA minus Lectra, in percentage points. A tie is within 0.1 point. Length saving is 100 × (1 − Lectra efficiency / BETA efficiency); positive means shorter. Numerical statistics use valid layouts only. A too_long failure is a loss with blank numerical scores. Other failures remain separate. The fixed-seed sample balances piece count and Lectra efficiency; its unweighted means are descriptive, not estimates for all customers.

A stronger analytic check uses each piece’s full source orientation set, including all mirror states, rather than BETA’s restricted set. It finds the minimum possible lengthwise span subject to fitting the stated width. The task lower bound is the largest piece bound. Support changes, interval ends and width-boundary roots are enumerated; this is a projection bound, not a nesting search. Source part joins and inverse transforms are checked. Tolerances: 1e-7 mm and 1e-6 in the length ratio.

Code-0 task 63488 is a small anomaly: Lectra’s implied length is 0.02916% below the analytic bound, equivalent to 0.01249 percentage point of efficiency. This is below the 0.1-point tie threshold. Coordinate precision or denominator details could explain it; that explanation remains unverified. It is not a gross width contradiction.

Four code-3 tasks contradict the ordinary-strip width model. The largest minimum required width in each task is 1.243–1.692 times the stated width. This supports excluding code 3 and is consistent with different sheet semantics, such as folded or tubular fabric. It does not identify the actual codebook. No code-2 task in this sample violates the analytic bound.

Correlation and geometry checks can falsify a gross width interpretation. They cannot prove the sheet-type codebook, nor separate every engine-quality gap from the imposed orientation handicap. Finite-length failures and remaining reference-denominator uncertainty must be considered.

For code 0, the positive correlations, 2.3556-point mean deficit and absence of a width-fit contradiction support this conditional comparison. The differences have wide tails, from -28.8761 to +37.1418 points. The sheet-type codebook and reference denominator remain unverified.
